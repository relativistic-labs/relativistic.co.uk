Symbol routes

Three routes from the design brief, drawn on a 16-unit, 45° grid and shown at 16, 32 and 128 px on ink and paper. The pixel view is the 16 px render enlarged six times. Below the routes: the palette and type applied to a sample hero.

01 / Routes

Decision: route A, the worldline r

  1. It passes every test in the brief. Built only from verticals, horizontals and one 45° stroke; every vertex sits on the 16-unit grid, so the stem and the notch are pixel-sharp at 16 and 32 px. One colour, no outline.
  2. It survives 16 px. The 3 px notch between the stem and the arm stays open in the tab, so it still reads as an r, in both themes.
  3. It carries the idea without explaining it. The stem is a worldline (time running upward); the arm is a light ray leaving it at exactly 45°. The arm's flat cut ends in a point, as the brief allows.
  4. It evolves what exists. The holding page's r and 45° arrow become one shape, so the change looks deliberate rather than a restart.
  5. B fails silhouette. A 90° cone is twice as wide as it is tall; at 16 px it is a dropdown chevron, which in a browser tab reads as interface, not a company.
  6. C fails at small sizes. The frame takes most of the pixels and the diagonal reads as a strike-through or an edit icon. "Contained" comes across as "forbidden".
